## Context
R70.9 states that Bengali translations are reviewed by someone who reads Bengali and that
machine-translated UI text is not shipped. The five `bn.yaml` values were authored inside story 1.9
and have not had that review — Dev flagged this openly rather than waiving it, which is the right
call. Nothing renders these strings yet, so there is no live harm, but the review must close before
any surface shows them to a user.

## Acceptance criteria

1. All keys in `lipikar/resources/strings/bn.yaml` are reviewed by a person who reads Bengali, and
   the reviewer is named in the story's completion notes.
2. `common.output.assistive_notice` is confirmed to carry the **same force** in Bengali as in
   English — that the output is a suggestion, that it awaits human verification, and that it must
   not be relied on for a legal purpose without checking the original deed. Weakened or ambiguous
   phrasing here is a defect, not a style preference (R00.6, R70.14).
3. `nazir.review.progress` is confirmed to read naturally with `{total}` before `{done}`, which is
   the reordering story 1.9 relies on to prove R70.10.
4. Domain terminology is confirmed appropriate for land-deed users: the register is the archaic
   revenue vocabulary of R20.6 (মৌজা, খতিয়ান, দাগ, দাখিলা …) rather than general modern Bengali,
   where those differ.
5. Any corrections keep the key set identical to `en.yaml` (R70.13) and remain NFC (R70.9); the
   existing tests stay green.
6. The review outcome — reviewer, date, and whether values changed — is recorded, so the next
   locale addition inherits a precedent rather than repeating this discovery.

## Technical notes

The five keys under review, as shipped:

```
common.output.assistive_notice
common.output.unverified_badge
nazir.review.progress
nazir.field.low_confidence_warning
fard.field.needs_verification
```

QA's own reading: the Bengali is genuine Bengali, not transliteration and not a copy of the English
(`test_bengali_is_a_real_translation_not_a_copy` passes, and the values are visibly distinct). It is
NFC on disk, UTF-8 without BOM, LF line endings — QA verified this at the byte level. What no
automated test can establish is whether a Bangladeshi reader finds the wording natural, correctly
registered, and — for the assistive notice — appropriately firm. That is what this story is for.

`assistive_notice` deserves the most attention. It is the sentence that tells a user not to rely on
this system's output for a legal purpose, it is referenced from a single key so it appears
identically everywhere (R70.14), and the users are people whose property rights are described by the
document on screen.

Reviewer note: this is a review of **UI copy**, not of deed data. No deed content should be shared
with the reviewer, and none is needed — the placeholders are `{field}`, `{done}`, `{total}`.
