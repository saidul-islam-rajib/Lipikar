## Test quality assessment

Good. Every test asserts exact values rather than shape, `test_summary_reports_the_whole_distribution`
checks all four statistics in one place with numbers I independently reproduced, and the tie-break
test supplies its inputs in the wrong order so it would fail on an unstable sort. All three
mutations were caught, including the one that fakes a distribution.

Two design decisions I checked and agree with:

- **Nearest-rank percentiles** (`reporting.py:50-55`) mean every reported percentile is an actually
  observed page score, not an interpolation between two pages. For "which pages are bad", an
  interpolated value would name a page that does not exist.
- **`percentiles: [90, 95]` excludes 50** because median has its own field. Reporting a `median` and
  a `p50` that differ by definition (0.45 vs 0.40 on the ten-sample fixture — I confirmed both
  numbers) would invite someone to read the gap as signal. The Dev section calls this out and it is
  the right call.

Minor gaps, not worth a finding on their own: no test for a single-sample set (where mean, median,
p90 and worst all coincide), and `worst_n` larger than the sample count is untested (the slice
handles it correctly — I checked `ranked[:config.worst_n]` cannot over-run).
